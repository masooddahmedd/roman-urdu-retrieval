\subsection{Script costs more than spelling}

Table~\ref{tab:wiki} shows the main result. Asked in Urdu script, the open embedders find the gold passage in the top five for \res{wiki/bge-m3/urdu}\% (bge-m3) and \res{wiki/e5/urdu}\% (mE5) of questions. Asked in Roman Urdu, the same questions reach \res{wiki/bge-m3/none}\% and \res{wiki/e5/none}\%. OpenAI's embeddings fall from \res{wiki/openai/urdu}\% to \res{wiki/openai/none}\%. Every dense model loses between \WikiDropMin{} and \WikiDropMax{} points, and BM25, which needs shared characters, falls from \res{wiki/bm25/urdu}\% to \res{wiki/bm25/none}\%.

Spelling matters less in aggregate: the three spellings of a question give Recall@5 within two points of each other for every retriever (Appendix~\ref{app:spellings}). The aggregate hides question-level instability, though. For \res{wiki/bge-m3/none/disagree}\% (bge-m3), \res{wiki/e5/none/disagree}\% (mE5) and \res{wiki/openai/none/disagree}\% (OpenAI) of questions, one spelling finds the passage and another misses it. A user who types \textit{main} instead of \textit{mein} can get a different answer.

\paragraph{Part of the drop is a language prior.} In the bilingual pool a Roman Urdu query often lands on English passages: they make up \res{wiki/bge-m3/enshare}\% of bge-m3's top five for Roman Urdu queries, \res{wiki/openai/enshare}\% of OpenAI's and \res{wiki/e5/enshare}\% of mE5's, although the gold passage is always Urdu. Removing the English passages (last column of Table~\ref{tab:wiki}) raises Roman Urdu Recall@5 to \res{wiki/bge-m3/pool}\%, \res{wiki/e5/pool}\% and \res{wiki/openai/pool}\%. The gap shrinks but does not close: with nothing but Urdu passages to choose from, bge-m3 still finds \res{wiki/bge-m3/pool}\% from Roman Urdu against \res{wiki/bge-m3/pool/urdu}\% from Urdu script. Latin-script Urdu looks like English to these models, and they also do not map it reliably onto Urdu-script meaning.

\paragraph{Human romanization shows the same gap.} On Belebele (Table~\ref{tab:belebele}), with an Urdu-only pool and Roman Urdu written by people, bge-m3 falls from \res{bb/bge-m3/urdu}\% to \res{bb/bge-m3/roman}\% and mE5 from \res{bb/e5/urdu}\% to \res{bb/e5/roman}\%, a drop of \BbDropMin{} to \BbDropMax{} points. Because Belebele's Roman Urdu questions are also reworded (Section~3), this is an upper bound on the script effect; it rules out the possibility that our Wikipedia result is an artefact of LLM-written spellings. English questions, by contrast, are retrieved about as well as Urdu-script ones (\res{bb/bge-m3/english}\% and \res{bb/e5/english}\%): the models handle a different language far better than the same language in a different script.

\subsection{Transliteration fixes most of it}

Table~\ref{tab:fixes} compares the fixes on the Wikipedia set.

\paragraph{Spelling rules hurt.} The \WikiNRules{} rules that raise agreement between spellings on the development questions lower Recall@5 for every dense model, to \res{wiki/bge-m3/normalize}\% for bge-m3, \res{wiki/e5/normalize}\% for mE5 and \res{wiki/openai/normalize}\% for OpenAI. Normalized text such as \textit{kya} for \textit{kia} and \textit{v} for \textit{w} makes spellings agree with each other but moves them away from anything the models saw in training.

\paragraph{LLM transliteration nearly reaches the Urdu-script ceiling.} Converting the query to Urdu script with gpt-4o-mini gives \res{wiki/bge-m3/translit}\% (bge-m3), \res{wiki/e5/translit}\% (mE5) and \res{wiki/bm25/translit}\% (BM25), against Urdu-script scores of \res{wiki/bge-m3/urdu}\%, \res{wiki/e5/urdu}\% and \res{wiki/bm25/urdu}\%. It also removes most spelling instability: questions on which the spellings disagree fall to \res{wiki/bge-m3/translit/disagree}\% and \res{wiki/e5/translit/disagree}\%. OpenAI's embeddings reach \res{wiki/openai/translit}\%, close to their own Urdu-script score of \res{wiki/openai/urdu}\%, and the hybrid takes them to \res{wiki/openai/translit_hybrid}\%, above it. For the open models the hybrid is within noise of transliteration alone.

\paragraph{A small transliteration model gets most of the way.} The m2m100 model reaches \res{wiki/bge-m3/translit_m2m}\% (bge-m3), \res{wiki/e5/translit_m2m}\% (mE5) and \res{wiki/bm25/translit_m2m}\% (BM25), recovering \MtwoMShare{} of the gain gpt-4o-mini gives the dense models, with no API call. Combined in the hybrid it reaches \res{wiki/bge-m3/translit_m2m_hybrid}\% and \res{wiki/e5/translit_m2m_hybrid}\%. The general-purpose open LLM Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct does worse (\res{wiki/bge-m3/translit_open}\% and \res{wiki/e5/translit_open}\%): \res{tq/wiki/qwen2.5-7b/foreign_script}\% of its outputs contain Devanagari, Cyrillic or other non-Arabic letters, and its outputs share \res{tq/wiki/qwen2.5-7b/word_jaccard}\% of their words with the Urdu question, against \res{tq/wiki/m2m100/word_jaccard}\% for m2m100 and \res{tq/wiki/gpt-4o-mini/word_jaccard}\% for gpt-4o-mini (Table~\ref{tab:translit}).

On Belebele the open transliterators help as well, raising bge-m3 from \res{bb/bge-m3/roman}\% to \res{bb/bge-m3/roman_translit_m2m}\% with m2m100 and to \res{bb/bge-m3/roman_translit_open}\% with Qwen2.5-7B, and mE5 from \res{bb/e5/roman}\% to \res{bb/e5/roman_translit_m2m}\% and \res{bb/e5/roman_translit_open}\%. The remaining gap to the Urdu-script questions is larger than on Wikipedia, as expected when the Roman Urdu question is also worded differently: transliteration changes the script, not the words.

\paragraph{Cost.} m2m100 took a median of \res{tq/sec/m2m100_cpu_median}~s per query on a laptop CPU (batch 16, beam 4), and Qwen2.5-7B \res{tq/sec/qwen2.5-7b_median}~s on a partly occupied consumer GPU. We did not record gpt-4o-mini latency. All three add a model call to every query; a deployed system would cache transliterations of frequent queries, use greedy decoding, or run the small model on a GPU.

\subsection{What still fails}

We compared, for each test question and spelling, the rank of the gold passage for the Urdu-script question, the raw Roman Urdu and its gpt-4o-mini transliteration. The transliterations are close to the original Urdu (mean word overlap \TrJaccard, identical words for \TrExact{} of them), and retrieval follows their quality: bge-m3 finds the passage for \ErrBgeHighOverlap\% of the \ErrNHighOverlap{} transliterations that share at least 80\% of their words with the Urdu question, and \ErrBgeLowOverlap\% of the \ErrNLowOverlap{} that share 50 to 80\%. Of bge-m3's \ErrBgeMiss{} misses after transliteration (out of \ErrBgePairs{} question--spelling pairs), \ErrBgeUrduAlso{} are also missed from Urdu script. We read the twelve misses whose transliteration is furthest from the Urdu question; they come from five questions. In four, a name ends up written differently from the passage: \textit{Ataliyi} (Italian) becomes an unrelated Urdu word and \textit{K2} stays in Latin script where the Urdu question spells it out; \textit{Nasir} is written as a different name; \textit{Gawar-Bati} is hyphenated and spelled differently; and \textit{Mohanjodaro} is split into two words. In the fifth, a technical word (the Urdu term for archaeology) is rendered as a common synonym. For OpenAI the picture differs: \ErrOpenaiUrduAlso{} of its \ErrOpenaiMiss{} misses after transliteration are also missed from Urdu script, so its remaining gap is its own weaker Urdu retrieval, not transliteration.
